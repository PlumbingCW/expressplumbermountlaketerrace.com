How a jetter works inside the pipe

A jetting machine pumps water at high pressure through a flexible hose. The nozzle at the end has jets that point backward, and those jets pull the hose forward along the pipe. Other jets spray outward and scour the pipe wall.

This is different from a cable, which pokes a hole through the clog. Jetting washes the buildup off the whole circumference and flushes it down the line. That is why jetting suits grease, sludge and silt, and why a root-cutting nozzle can handle roots that would just grow back after a plain cable.

Names and gas: clearing up the confusion

Hydro jetting, hydrojetting and hydra-jetting are one method. It uses water only. No gas is burned, and nothing flammable goes into the pipe.

A sewer-gas smell near a drain is a different thing. It often means a dry P-trap or a blockage that traps odor. If you smell natural gas, with its rotten-egg odor, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before you call us.

Old buildings, unit by unit and scheduled maintenance
