Why Your Mountlake Terrace Drain Smells and What to Check First

A smelly drain in Mountlake Terrace usually comes from one of four sources, and only one of them is a true sewer problem. This guide sorts a dry P-trap, a biofilm coating, a vent fault and sewer gas so you know what to check first.

Not every bad smell is a sewer problem

The first question is which drain smells and when. One fixture that smells only when you run water is a local problem, often a dirty stopper or a film inside the trap. A smell that drifts through the house with no water running points to a dry trap, a vent, or the line itself.

Most smelly drains we hear about turn out to be simple. A dry trap or a coating on the pipe wall explains the majority, and both are cheap to fix at home. The serious causes are rarer but worth knowing, because a sewer line problem gets worse the longer it sits.

The dry P-trap, the simplest culprit

Every sink, tub, shower and floor drain has a curved section called a P-trap. It stays full of water, and that water is the seal that keeps sewer gas out of the room. When a fixture sits unused, the seal evaporates.

The fix is to run water for about a minute. For a floor drain or a seldom-used shower, a spoonful of cooking oil on top of the refilled trap slows evaporation, though it is not a substitute for using the fixture now and then. If the trap keeps going dry even with regular use, suspect siphoning, which brings us to venting.

Biofilm, the film you smell but cannot see

Soap, skin oils and food residue build a slimy layer inside the trap and on the underside of a stopper or overflow opening. Bacteria feed on it and give off a sour or swampy smell. This is the most common cause of a bathroom sink that smells even though it drains fast.

Pull the stopper and wipe it with a bottle brush, then clean the overflow hole near the top of the basin. Follow with hot tap water and a squirt of dish soap. A kitchen sink needs the same treatment on the strainer and the rubber splash baffle if you have a disposal.

A ten-minute way to find the smelly fixture

When you cannot tell where the smell lives, work through the house in order.

01

Run water in every unused drain

Fill each trap, including the basement floor drain and the shower nobody uses, and wait ten minutes.

02

Sniff each fixture up close

Put your nose near the drain opening with the water off. The strongest source is your target.

03

Clean that fixture by hand

Remove the stopper, scrub the visible parts and flush with hot water and a little dish soap.

04

Watch for gurgling

Run the sink full, then release it and listen. A gurgle means air is fighting for a path, which points to a vent or partial clog.

05

Check the cleanout

If the smell is strongest near a capped pipe in the basement, garage or yard, leave the cap on and call us.

Vent trouble and why it brings the smell indoors

Plumbing vents run up through the roof so air can enter the drain lines behind the water flow. A blocked vent stack, from leaves, a nest or frost, lets the water pull the trap seal along with it, and the room fills with gas. The tell is a gurgling drain, a toilet that bubbles when the washer drains, and a smell that comes and goes.

You can look at the roof opening from the ground with binoculars, but do not climb a wet roof. A loose or cracked vent joint inside a wall can also leak gas into a cavity, which is why a smell sometimes seems to come from a wall rather than a drain.

Safe things to try today

These steps are low risk and cover most surface causes.

  • ✓Refill every trap. Run water for one minute in each unused fixture.
  • ✓Clean stoppers and overflow holes. A bottle brush reaches where a cloth cannot.
  • ✓Flush with hot tap water. Not boiling water, which can soften plastic traps and fittings.
  • ✓Run the disposal with cold water and citrus peel. It scrubs the chamber walls a little.
  • ✓Open a window and run the bath fan. Clear the air while you track the source.
  • ✓Leave chemical drain cleaners alone. They can harm pipes and make a later repair more hazardous.

Water heater smell is a separate problem

If only the hot tap smells like rotten eggs, the odor comes from bacteria reacting with the anode rod inside the tank, not from your drains. Cold water without a smell confirms it. A water heater visit, not a drain visit, is the right call there.

When a drain smell is serious, and what to do

Stop using the affected fixtures if sewage appears in a tub or floor drain, if several toilets will not flush, or if the smell is strong enough to cause a headache. Move children and pets out of a basement that smells strongly. Do not mix cleaning products to fight the odor.

A rotten-egg smell that seems to come from an appliance or the air itself could be natural gas. Leave the building, then call your gas utility or 911 from outside.

Questions about drain smells

The P-trap under every fixture holds a small plug of water that blocks gas from the drain line. In a guest bath or a basement floor drain that water evaporates over weeks. Run water for a minute and add a little more to refill it, and the smell often stops.

A faint whiff now and then is mostly a nuisance, but a strong or constant smell should not be ignored. Open windows and stop using the fixture. If you also feel dizzy or get headaches, leave the home and call 911 or your gas utility if the smell is like rotten eggs, since natural gas carries a similar odor.
