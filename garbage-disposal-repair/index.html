Does it hum, stay silent or leak? Start there

Those three sounds point to three different repairs, and you can tell them apart in a minute. We answer the phone 24/7, so if the kitchen is flooding or the unit has come off the sink, call now and keep the water off while you wait.

A hum with no spin means the blades are stuck, often from a bone, a fruit pit or a bottle cap. A silent unit has either tripped its overload or lost power, or the motor has burned out. A leak is a seal, a seam or a drain connection letting go.

Do the safe checks first. Turn the wall switch off, and then look under the sink to see where the water originates.

Before you touch it, what to do and what to leave alone

A disposal can injure you or shock you if you treat it casually.

  • ✓Switch it off, then unplug it. Most units plug in under the sink. If yours is wired directly, turn off the breaker only when the panel is dry.
  • ✓Never put your hand in the drain. Use tongs or pliers to lift out an object you can see, and only with the power off.
  • ✓Try the hex socket underneath. A hex wrench in the center hole on the bottom lets you rock a stuck flywheel free. Then press the red reset button.
  • ✓Skip drain chemicals. They sit in the chamber, burn skin and can eat the seals.
  • ✓Stay clear of wet outlets. A leak that reaches a plug or switch is a shock risk. Leave it and call.
  • ✓Do not run it with a leak. Running a leaking disposal pushes more water out and soaks the cabinet floor.

What a disposal repair or swap involves

We shut off power and water first. For a jam, we free the flywheel and test the motor under load. For a leak, we find where the water comes from. A cracked housing means a new unit, while a loose mount or a dried gasket can often be repaired.

A replacement means releasing the mounting ring, lifting out the old unit, and seating a new one on the sink flange with fresh putty or a gasket. We reconnect the dishwasher drain, rebuild the P-trap and test with the water running.

A good hookup includes the dishwasher knockout plug, which must be removed on a new unit. Forgetting it is a common reason a fresh disposal does not drain the dishwasher.

Why disposals back up past the trap

The disposal grinds food into small pieces, but it does not carry them away. Water does that, and grease sticks to the pipe wall beyond the trap. Over months the scraps build up into a plug. Run cold water for a while after you grind, and keep fats out of the sink.

If the sink backs up even when the disposal is empty, the blockage is downstream and calls for drain cleaning rather than a new disposal.
