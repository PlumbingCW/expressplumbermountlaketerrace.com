Which Toilet Part Is Broken? A Mountlake Terrace Parts Guide

Most toilet trouble comes from a handful of cheap parts inside the tank and under the bowl, not from the toilet itself. This guide to toilet repair parts in Mountlake Terrace shows which part matches which symptom, and what to leave to a plumber.

By the Mountlake Terrace Express Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026

You probably do not need a new toilet

A toilet that runs, hisses, rocks or leaks at the base nearly always has one worn part. The porcelain lasts for decades, while the rubber and plastic inside it wear out in a few years.

That is the short answer. Replace the part that matches the symptom, and replace the toilet only if the bowl or tank is cracked. The table below pairs each common symptom with its likely part.

The flapper and the fill valve

The flapper is the rubber seal at the bottom of the tank. When it ages, it stops sealing and lets a thin stream leak into the bowl, which tells the fill valve to top up the tank. That is the intermittent refill you hear at night.

The fill valve is the tall assembly that shuts off the incoming water when the float reaches the set level. When it wears or sticks, water keeps running into the overflow tube. Both parts are sold as universal kits, but match the flush valve size and the tank brand when you can.

Replace both at once if the toilet is more than a few years old. They wear together, and the second repair costs you another trip.

Swapping a flapper in four moves

This is a good first repair if you want to try one yourself.

01

Shut the water and drain the tank

Close the supply stop by the wall, flush, and hold the handle until the tank is mostly empty.

02

Unhook the old flapper

Lift the ears off the posts on the overflow tube and unclip the chain. Take it to the store so the new one matches.

03

Seat the new one

Set the ears on the posts and clip the chain with a bit of slack, so the flapper can drop fully closed.

04

Test with dye

Add food coloring to the tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper still leaks.

What to have on hand before you start

A repair goes faster when the parts and the tools are on the floor before the water is off.

A flapper and a fill valve

Buy a matched pair, or a kit that lists your flush valve size on the box.

A new braided supply line

Measure from the shutoff valve to the tank inlet and pick a length with a little slack.

A sponge and a small towel

There is always a cup of water left in the tank, and the supply line holds more.

Adjustable pliers and a flat screwdriver

Hand-tight plus a quarter turn is enough on plastic nuts. A wrench used hard cracks them.

Food coloring

A few drops in the tank before and after the repair shows if the flapper seals.

Supply line, shutoff valve and tank bolts

A braided supply line connects the shutoff valve to the fill valve. When it kinks, corrodes or loosens, you get a puddle behind the toilet or a damp wall. Replace the line, and hand-tighten the nuts before a final quarter turn with a wrench.

The shutoff valve, often called the supply stop, can also drip at the stem. If the stop will not close, you cannot do the other repairs safely. A stuck or leaking stop is a plumber job, so call (206) 864-6558 before forcing it.

The two bolts that hold the tank to the bowl have rubber washers that fail over time. A drip under the tank usually means one of them. Tighten evenly and gently, because porcelain cracks under too much force.

When the problem is under the toilet

The wax ring seals the toilet outlet to the flange in the floor. When it fails, water seeps out at the base, the floor under the toilet softens, or a sewer smell shows up in the bathroom. The flange itself can crack or sit too low.

That repair means lifting the toilet, replacing the seal, and checking the floor and the flange. If water has been leaking for a while, a leak check may be in order too. See leak detection for how we find hidden water.

When to stop and call

  • ✓Water at the base of the toilet. The wax seal may have failed.
  • ✓A soft or stained floor near the toilet. Water may be getting into the subfloor.
  • ✓A shutoff valve that will not close. Do not force it. Call us.
  • ✓A cracked tank or bowl. Shut the supply and call for a replacement plan.
  • ✓Wet outlets or lights near a leak. Do not touch them. Shut the breaker only if you can reach it dry and safely.

A note on flush strength and slow drains

A weak flush is not always a tank part. If water rises in the bowl and drains slowly, the trap or the branch drain behind it may be partly blocked, and a new flapper will not change that. Plunge with a flange plunger first, with the bowl at normal level.

If plunging fails, or other drains in the house gurgle at the same time, the blockage is further down the line. Stop flushing and call. Another flush can push water over the rim.
