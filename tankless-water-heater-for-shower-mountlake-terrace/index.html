Will a Tankless Heater Keep Your Mountlake Terrace Shower Hot?

Yes, a tankless water heater for a shower in Mountlake Terrace works well when it is sized to the flow you actually use. It falls short when two showers, a dishwasher and a cold winter all hit it at once.

By the Mountlake Terrace Express Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026

How a shower decides what size heater you need

A tankless heater has two limits. One is how much water per minute it can heat. The other is how many degrees it can raise that water. Your shower asks for a flow and a final temperature, and the heater must reach both.

On a mild day the incoming water is cooler than room temperature but not cold. In winter it is colder, so the heater has to add more heat to reach the same shower temperature. At the same setting, the unit hits its limit sooner.

So the short answer is yes for a single shower, if the heater is sized for your coldest months. Add a second shower, a laundry load or a dishwasher, and you have to count the flow of every fixture that can run together.

Size it yourself in four steps

You can do this on paper before you call anyone.

01

List the fixtures that run together

Count the showers, sinks, dishwasher and washer that might run at the same time.

02

Find each flow rating

Check the shower head, the faucet aerators and the appliance labels.

03

Add them up

The total is the flow the heater must handle. Do not count fixtures that never overlap.

04

Plan for winter

Assume the coldest inlet water of the year, not the average. Ask the installer to size for that.

The minimum flow problem

Tankless units need a minimum flow before the burner or the elements turn on. A very gentle shower, or a faucet opened just a crack, may sit below that line. The heater never fires, and you get cold water from a tap you thought was hot.

This shows up most at a bathroom sink, where people run a trickle to wash hands. It also shows up with some ultra low-flow shower heads. Check the heater's minimum activation flow in its manual and compare it to the lowest flow you use.

Shower valves matter too. A pressure balancing or thermostatic valve in the wall keeps the temperature steady when someone flushes a toilet. An old valve can make a heater seem unstable when the valve is the part at fault.

What happens when two things run at once

If the demand is higher than the unit can heat, one of two things happens. The water comes out cooler, or the heater limits the flow to hold the temperature. Either way, the person in the shower feels it.

Low-flow shower heads, a small dishwasher cycle at a different time, and a laundry load delayed until after showers all help. These fixes cost only a change in habit.

If the household has several bathrooms and heavy use at the same hour, a second unit or a larger one may be the better plan. See water heater installation for how that decision is made.

The cold water sandwich

Some people notice a burst of cold water between two short showers or in the middle of a long one. The heater shuts off, the water in the pipe cools, and the next draw has to wait for the heater to restart.

Short, frequent draws make this worse. It is normal behavior, and a recirculation setup or a heater with a small buffer can soften it.

Should you wait for a failure or plan ahead

If your current tank still works, you have time to pick the heater, price the gas or electrical work, and choose the controls. A heater that dies on a cold weekend gets replaced in a hurry, with fewer choices.

A heater that leaks or has rust at the tank base cannot wait. Shut off the cold supply to it and call. See our tankless water heater page for what the switch involves, and call (206) 864-6558 if you would like to talk through the sizing first.

What this means in Mountlake Terrace

Puget Sound winters bring colder water to the heater's inlet. A tankless unit has to raise that water further, so output drops in winter. A heater that fills a shower well in September may feel tight in January. Size the unit for the cold months, not the mild ones, so winter showers do not turn lukewarm.

Washington adopts the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code with state amendments. For you, that means the gas piping, the venting and the connections have to meet code, and the installer should size them to the unit. Ask how the vent route and the gas line will be sized before you sign off on a heater.
