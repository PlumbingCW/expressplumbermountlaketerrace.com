No water, or too much of it in the yard

A failed service line shows up two ways. Either the taps go dry or run weak, or water surfaces where it should not, usually a soft patch in the lawn or a wet strip along the driveway. Both mean urgent work, and our 24/7 line is open now.

While you wait, close the house main so you know the leak is not inside, stay off soft ground over the line, and keep kids and pets away from any hole that is forming. Take a photo of the wet spot and note when you first saw it.

Who owns which stretch of pipe

The City of Mountlake Terrace provides water inside city limits. It supplies nearly 2 million gallons of drinking water per day to more than 6,000 homes and businesses. The city's mains run under the street and the meter sits at the property edge.

Where the city's responsibility ends and yours begins can differ by property, so ask City Public Works at 425-670-8264, Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., before anyone digs. After hours use the Snohomish County non-emergency line at 425-407-3999.

Once it is established that the break is on your side, we handle everything from the meter to the house. If the break is the city's, we do not dig in its line, and we will say so on the first call.

The line material nobody wrote down

Homes from different decades have different service lines. Older lines may be lead, galvanized steel or copper, while newer ones are often plastic. If you are not sure what is under your yard, say so when you call.

A line of unknown material that has already leaked once is a good candidate for full replacement. Patching one failure on a worn pipe tends to move the next one down the line. If the lead question is on your mind, ask us about it and we explain what to check and what the options are.

Signs the service line is the problem

These clues sit outside the house, which is why many owners miss them.

  • ✓Meter turns with everything off. This is the clearest sign when the house main is closed.
  • ✓Soft, sunken or green ground. Water saturates soil and feeds grass faster over the break.
  • ✓Low pressure at every fixture. A leak before the house steals flow from all taps.
  • ✓Dirty or sputtering water. Air or silt can enter a damaged line.
  • ✓Hissing at the meter box. Pressurized water escaping makes a steady sound.
  • ✓A sudden jump in the water bill. The city bills every other month, so a leak can run a full cycle before the statement shows it.

Repair the line alone or repipe everything

Some owners fix the service line and discover the pipes inside are just as tired. If the house is old, ask us whether it makes sense to do both while crews are on site. A new line feeding old galvanized pipe inside still gives you rusty water and weak pressure.

That conversation leads to whole-house repiping, which is a bigger project but one that is handled once. If you only need the line, we do only the line. It is your choice.
