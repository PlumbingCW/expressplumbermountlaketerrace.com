Frozen and Burst Pipes in Mountlake Terrace: Prevent, Thaw, Shut Off

If a faucet runs a trickle on a freezing morning, a pipe in your Mountlake Terrace home is probably frozen. Shut the main valve if you see any water leaking, thaw only with gentle warmth, and call us if the pipe has split.

Water is leaking from a frozen pipe right now

A pipe that has split lets go as it thaws. Move fast, but safely.

  • 1Close the main shutoff valve at the house. A quarter turn closes a ball valve, and a gate valve takes several turns.
  • 2Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain what is left in the lines.
  • 3Switch off power to any area with standing water only if the breaker panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Otherwise stay out and call us.
  • 4Move belongings away from the wet area and catch drips with buckets.
  • 5Call (206) 864-6558. Tell the dispatcher where the water is and whether the main is shut.

Why pipes freeze where they do

Water expands as it freezes, and a plastic or metal pipe has nowhere for that growth to go. The pipe often does not break at the ice. It splits where pressure builds between the ice block and a closed faucet, which is why leaks appear after thawing.

Pipes freeze first where cold air reaches them and warm air does not. In a house that means exterior walls, vented crawlspaces, garages, the attic and anything running near a leaky rim joist. A hose left on a hose bib is a classic case, because it traps water and keeps the cold in contact with the fixture.

Thawing a frozen pipe without making things worse

Thaw only when no water is leaking and the pipe looks intact.

01

Open the faucet

Open the tap on the frozen line so melting water has a place to go.

02

Apply gentle warmth

Use a hair dryer or warm towels, starting at the faucet end and working back toward the frozen spot.

03

Never use flame

No torch, no kerosene heater pointed at the pipe, no heat gun on high. They can ignite framing or burst the pipe.

04

Watch for leaks

As flow returns, check the whole run. If you see water at the pipe wall, close the main and call.

05

Find the cause

Once it thaws, add insulation or seal the draft. Otherwise the pipe will freeze again.

Before the forecast drops

  • ✓Hoses off. Disconnect and drain every outdoor hose and shut any interior hose bib stop.
  • ✓Know the main. Test your shutoff valve now and label it.
  • ✓Wrap the exposed pipes. Foam sleeves on crawlspace, garage and exterior-wall lines.
  • ✓Seal drafts. Foam any gap where a pipe enters the house, and close crawlspace vents if yours have covers.
  • ✓Open cabinets. Leave sink doors open on exterior walls overnight.
  • ✓Plan for time away. If you travel, keep the heat on above a cool setting and ask someone to check the house.

Know your shutoff before the cold arrives

Every home has a main shutoff where the supply enters the house. Find it in daylight, turn it off and on once, and tag it. A valve that has never been moved can stick, and a frozen-pipe leak is a bad time to find out.

Many homes also have fixture stops under sinks and behind toilets. Those let you cut off one line without losing water everywhere. If a stop weeps or will not turn, that is a quick repair now and a flood risk later.

For a split pipe inside a wall or crawlspace, shutting the main is the whole job until we arrive. Do not try to patch a pipe that is under pressure, and do not cut open a ceiling that is holding water. Call us and let the dispatcher walk you through what to do.

A thawed pipe is not always a safe pipe

A pipe that has frozen once can be weakened, even when it does not leak. The ice may have stressed a joint or the pipe wall. If a line froze solid and then thawed, watch it closely for a few days and look at joints and valves for weeping.

If a line freezes repeatedly in the same spot, no amount of wrapping fixes the underlying problem. It may need rerouting or a larger repair, and that is a conversation for our dispatcher. Our water line repair page explains what that work involves.

Call right away if

Call at once if a pipe is spraying, if water is coming through a ceiling, or if there is water near the electrical panel or outlets. Call if the water pressure drops across the house and the main is open, or if the water heater discharge is dripping steadily.
