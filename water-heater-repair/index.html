Water pooling under the tank right now

If water is spreading from your heater, you do not need to wait for a repair to limit the damage. We run a 24/7 emergency line, and while you wait for the visit there are a few things you can do to keep the leak small.

Gas units come first. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else. We do not service gas lines, and a gas smell is never a reason to stay inside.

  • 1Close the cold-water supply valve on the pipe entering the top of the tank. Turn the handle clockwise until it stops. A lever handle closes when it sits crosswise to the pipe.
  • 2On an electric unit, switch off its breaker only if the panel area is dry and you can reach it safely. If water is near the panel or outlets, stay away and tell the dispatcher.
  • 3On a gas unit with no gas smell, turn the gas control knob to the off or pilot setting. Do not touch the burner compartment.
  • 4Place a bucket or a wet vac under any drip, and lift stored boxes and carpet off the floor. Keep cords out of the water.

Fixable leak or finished tank

Water on the floor is the most common reason people call, and it is also the most misread. A wet tank is not always a failed tank. Condensation on a cold-water pipe, a loose union on the top, or a drip from the T&P valve discharge pipe can all look like a leak from the heater itself.

The test is where the water starts. If it runs from a fitting, a valve or a pipe connection, a repair usually makes sense. If it seeps from the tank's jacket or collects under the insulation, the steel liner has failed. No part can fix that, and we will say so rather than sell you a repair that will not hold.

Callers often find water in a garage, closet or attic instead of a basement. The tank drains toward whatever is lowest, so the puddle can sit well away from the heater. Following the damp line back to the heater is part of the diagnosis.

No hot water: pilots, elements and thermostats

A heater that makes no hot water at all is a different job from a leaker. On a gas unit, the pilot flame heats a thermocouple that tells the gas valve it is safe to open. When that sensor wears out, the pilot goes out and the burner never fires. Replacing a thermocouple is a small repair on a unit that is otherwise sound.

Electric units heat with two elements, one near the top of the tank and one near the bottom. If the top element fails you get no hot water. If the bottom one fails you get a short supply that runs cold fast. A tripped high-limit button, the small red reset on the upper thermostat, can also cut power, and it usually trips for a reason.

If you smell gas near a gas heater, do not try to relight anything. Leave the building, call the gas utility or 911, and call us once you are safe. We do not work on gas supply lines, but we will check the heater once the utility clears the building.

What a repair visit covers

Every visit starts with finding the actual fault, not swapping parts and hoping. Here is what we typically check.

  • ✓The leak path. We dry the area and trace the water to its source before we name a repair.
  • ✓The T&P valve and discharge pipe. A valve that weeps or a pipe that is capped or kinked is a safety problem and gets addressed first.
  • ✓Drain valve and supply connections. Worn washers, loose unions and corroded nipples are common sources.
  • ✓Anode rod. This sacrificial rod protects the tank steel. A spent rod is one reason older tanks fail early.
  • ✓Heating parts. Thermocouple, igniter and gas control on gas units, elements and thermostats on electric ones.
  • ✓Venting and combustion air. Gas units need a clear flue and enough air, so we check for backdrafting and blockage.

What changes the price of a water heater repair

Two repairs that look alike can cost very different amounts. These are the things that move the number, and we explain which ones apply to your unit.

Which part failed

A thermocouple, a drain valve and a gas control valve are very different parts with very different labor.

Fuel type and unit style

Gas, electric, power-vent and hybrid heat pump models each have their own parts and test steps.

Access to the unit

A tank in an open utility area is quick to reach. One in a tight closet, attic or crawlspace takes longer.

Age and condition of the tank

Rusted fittings and seized valves can turn a small repair into a larger one, and sometimes into a replacement.

Time of the call

A same-day or after-hours visit is priced differently from a scheduled one.

Homeowners policies usually cover sudden accidental damage, such as a tank that bursts and floods a room. They usually do not cover wear, corrosion or a slow leak. Check your own policy. We give a clear price before work starts, so call (206) 864-6558 for your number.

When repairing stops making sense

A tank that has reached its mid-teens, shows rust at the connections and keeps needing parts is telling you something. Each repair buys a little time on a steel shell that is wearing out from the inside. When the shell fails, the repair money is gone along with the tank.

Our rule of thumb is simple. If the failed part is separate from the tank and the tank is sound, repair it. If the tank itself is leaking, or it is old and has several problems at once, look at a replacement water heater instead. We lay out both paths in plain terms and do not push either one.

If you are curious about switching styles, a tankless unit heats on demand and has no tank to rust through. It is a bigger change than a repair, so read about tankless water heater service before deciding.

Strapping, quakes and finding your door

Mountlake Terrace sits in earthquake country, and a full water heater is heavy and tall. Straps or a seismic kit anchor the tank to the wall studs so it cannot tip and tear the gas or water lines loose. If your tank stands loose in a garage or closet, ask us to check the straps while we are there.

Geography matters for dispatch too. The city sits along Interstate 5, with interchanges at 220th Street SW, 236th Street SW and SR 104, and SR 99 forms the western edge. When you call, give the dispatcher your cross street and which side of I-5 you are on, so we can find the right door fast.

Error codes and newer units

Newer heaters report faults on a small display. A code usually names a sensor, an igniter, a blocked vent or a condensate drain that has backed up. The code tells us where to look. It does not tell us the part has failed, so we test before we replace anything.
