Will a tankless heater fit your house?

A tankless heater heats water as it flows, so it has no tank to fill, rust or flood. The tradeoff is that it needs a lot of fuel or power all at once. A gas unit may need a larger gas line than the tank it replaces. An electric unit may need heavy wiring your panel cannot spare.

That is why we want to see the site before giving a price. We look at the gas meter and line, the venting route, the electrical panel and a place for the condensate to drain. If a tank swap does not make sense, we say so, and a conventional tank stays a fair option.

What an annual tankless tune-up checks

A yearly visit finds small problems before they become a no hot water call.

  • ✓Error history. Past codes show repeating faults, such as flame or flow problems.
  • ✓Heat exchanger scale. We check how much scale has built up and whether a flush is due.
  • ✓Inlet filter screen. A clogged screen cuts flow and triggers shutdowns.
  • ✓Vent pipe and terminations. Joints should be tight, sloped properly and clear of blockage.
  • ✓Condensate line. On condensing units, a blocked drain stops the unit.
  • ✓Ignition and flame sensor. Dirty sensors cause lockouts and cold-water bursts.
  • ✓Temperature and flow. We test output at a tap to confirm the unit performs.

Flush and descale: what it is and why it matters

Water carries dissolved minerals, and heating it deposits them as scale on the inside of the heat exchanger. Scale acts like insulation, so the burner works harder, the water comes out lukewarm and the unit may overheat and shut itself off.

A flush uses a pump, two hoses and a descaling solution to circulate through the exchanger, followed by a clean-water rinse. It is a service job, not a replacement job. If you were told the only option was a new unit, ask for a flush and an inspection first.

Mineral content varies between water supplies, so ask your water provider for its report if you want to know how fast scale may form in your unit.

Winter, wet season and earthquake country

Puget Sound has a wet season from fall to spring and occasional winter freezes. A tankless unit hung on an outside wall, or one with exposed supply lines in a garage or crawlspace, can freeze, and a vent that fills with water or debris can fault the unit. Check the supply lines and vent termination before the first cold snap.

The region is also earthquake country. A wall-mounted unit should be fixed firmly to studs, and the gas connector should have enough slack to flex. We look at both during a service visit.

If you smell gas near any gas unit, leave and call the gas utility or 911 first. Then call us.

What changes the price of tankless service or a swap

A flush is a different project from converting a tank. These factors apply.

Type of work

A flush, a repair, a tune-up and a full swap are priced very differently.

Gas line size

A tankless unit often needs a bigger gas line, which adds piping.

Venting and condensate

New vent pipe, a wall penetration and a drain for condensate each add work.

Electrical needs

Electric units need heavy circuits, and condensing gas units need power for the fan.

Access and condition

Tight locations and corroded isolation valves add time.
