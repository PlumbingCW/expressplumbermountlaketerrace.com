How a pinpoint survey narrows the search

We start by sorting the problem into one of three zones: supply lines under pressure, drain lines, or water coming from outside the structure. A closed-system pressure test on the supply side tells us whether the leak is on that side at all.

Next comes listening. A sensitive microphone on the floor, wall or valve picks up the hiss of escaping water, and the sound is loudest directly over the leak. Moisture meters and thermal imaging back that up by showing cooler, wetter material behind a finish. Where the pipe is hot, a warm patch on the slab gives it away.

When two methods point to the same spot, we mark it on the surface. That mark is what keeps the opening small, whether it is a drywall patch or a saw cut in concrete.

Run the all-fixtures-off meter test

This takes a few minutes and tells you if there is a leak at all. Tell the dispatcher what you saw.

01

Stop all water use

Close every tap, make sure no one flushes, and pause the dishwasher, washer, ice maker and irrigation.

02

Find the meter

It sits in a box near the street or property line. Lift the lid carefully and wipe the glass.

03

Read the dial and the small leak indicator

A spinning triangle or wheel with everything off means water is moving somewhere.

04

Wait, then compare

Check again after the wait. If the numbers changed, close the main and see whether the dial stops. If it stops, the leak is inside your side of the system.

When the water bill climbs and nothing looks wet

A quiet toilet is the most common hidden cause. A worn flapper lets water slip into the bowl all day with no sound, and the meter never stops. Irrigation valves stuck open and an outdoor spigot dripping into a bed do the same thing without any damp inside the house.

The City of Mountlake Terrace supplies water, sewer and stormwater inside city limits and bills every other month. That means a leak can run for a full billing period before the bill shows it. Read your meter yourself between bills, since a dial that is moving at night tells you sooner than a statement does.

If a leak has already pushed a bill up, City Hall utility billing at 425-744-6214 can explain the discount program for households under the county income limit and whether a payment plan fits. We fix the leak, and they handle the account.

Slab leaks and the warning signs on the floor

A slab leak is a pressurized line leaking beneath the concrete floor. Warning signs include a warm spot underfoot, damp baseboards, carpet that stays wet, the sound of running water with nothing on, and a water bill that jumps without a visible reason.

The hot line is the usual suspect when a floor feels warm. We confirm which line is losing pressure before anyone breaks concrete.

Once the spot is marked, there are choices. Saw-cutting the slab reaches the pipe directly. Rerouting a new line through the attic or walls avoids the floor entirely. A repipe replaces the supply lines throughout. We explain each path, who restores the flooring afterward, and give a clear price before work starts. For a house-wide option, see whole-house repiping.

Signs of a leak inside walls, ceilings and crawlspaces

Hidden leaks leave clues if you know where to look.

  • ✓Stains that grow after rain-free days. A stain that keeps spreading when the weather is dry points to plumbing, not the roof.
  • ✓Paint that bubbles or wallpaper that lifts. Moisture is pushing from behind the finish.
  • ✓A bathroom above the stain. Shower pans, tub drains, toilet seals and P-traps leak down into the ceiling below.
  • ✓Low pressure at a few fixtures. A leak ahead of the fixtures steals flow from them.
  • ✓Sewer smell near the floor. A cracked or loose drain under the house can leak gas and water into the crawlspace.

Buried lines between the meter and the house

Water surfacing in a yard, a soft patch of ground, or a strip of unusually green grass points to a leak on the private side of the line. Pipe tracing and listening let us narrow it down without opening the whole run.

What changes the price of leak detection

Detection pricing follows the effort and tools the search needs. We do not quote a number sight unseen.

Size and layout of the property

More bathrooms, floors and pipe runs mean more places to test and listen.

Where the leak is

A visible bathroom ceiling leak is quicker than one under concrete or a long buried line.

Pipe material

Metal carries sound well. Plastic and PEX need more pressure isolation work.

Number of methods needed

Some leaks need acoustic work plus thermal imaging or moisture readings to confirm.

Repair after the find

Opening a wall, cutting a slab or digging a yard adds labor, and restoration is a separate question.

Homeowners policies usually cover sudden accidental damage, like a burst pipe, and usually do not cover wear, corrosion or slow leaks. Check your own policy before you call a claim in. We give a clear price before work starts, so call (206) 864-6558 for your number.
