Stop the flow, then make one phone call

A burst is an emergency, and our line is open 24/7. While you wait, your job is simple: close the main, kill nearby power only if it is safe, and move what you can out of the water.

Speed matters because a broken supply line delivers a steady flow under pressure. The sooner the main is closed, the less drywall, insulation and flooring gets soaked. The dispatcher stays on the line until you know the water is under control.

A pipe is spraying or flooding right now

Follow these in order. Stop at any step where you are not safe.

  • 1Close the main shutoff valve inside the house. If you cannot find it or it will not turn, do not force it.

  • 4Stay out of rooms where water is near outlets, a panel or appliances. Do not touch electrical gear while standing in water.

The cause shapes the repair. A freeze split behaves differently from corrosion.

How we repair a broken line

We confirm the line is isolated and drained, then find the exact break. Water travels along pipe and framing, so the wet spot is often not where the failure sits.

We open only what we need. For a pinhole or a split fitting, that means cutting out the damaged piece and installing new pipe with proper fittings. When the pipe around the break is thin, scaled or soft, we show you and discuss replacing a longer run instead of patching it.

Then we restore pressure, check every joint for weeping, and confirm the fixtures work. You get a clear price before work starts, and we explain if drywall or flooring will need a separate trade afterward.

Leaks that look different from a classic burst

Not every break sprays. A pipe under the house after other work, a slow weep behind a shower valve, and water behind a garage refrigerator supply line all start small. A bubble in the paint, a sound of running water in a quiet house, or a stain that keeps growing can all be a burst in disguise.

If you cannot see where the water is coming from, we locate it first. Our emergency leak detection work pinpoints the pipe before anyone opens a wall. And when a whole system keeps failing, replacing the old supply lines is the question to ask us about.

What causes pipes to burst, and what you can check today

Age, cold, pressure and past workmanship account for most breaks.

  • ✓Freezing. Water expands as it turns to ice, and the split often shows up when the line thaws.
  • ✓Age and corrosion. Old galvanized and thin copper wear from the inside until a pinhole appears.
  • ✓High water pressure. A failed pressure regulator on the main can stress every joint in the house.
  • ✓Shifted or unsupported pipe. Pipes that rub framing or hang loose crack at fittings over time.
  • ✓Poor soldering or glue work. A joint made without enough heat or cleaning can fail years later.
  • ✓Water hammer. A slamming valve sends a shock through the line, so a hammer arrestor helps.

Winter in the Puget Sound lowers the margin

The Puget Sound has a wet fall-to-spring season and occasional winter freezes. The pipes that suffer are the ones outside the heated envelope: hose bibs, crawlspace lines and garage supply runs, plus any pipe in an exterior wall without insulation.

Before a cold snap, disconnect hoses, shut off and drain exterior spigots if you have an interior shutoff, and keep garage and crawlspace vents closed. If a pipe freezes, close the main before it thaws. Do not use a torch or open flame, since that starts fires and can crack the pipe.
