Bathroom Remodel Plumbing in Mountlake Terrace: Decisions Before Demo

The plumbing for a bathroom remodel in Mountlake Terrace gets decided before the first tile comes off. Fixture locations, drain pitch, venting and valve choices are all fixed once the walls close, so settle them while the studs are open.

By the Mountlake Terrace Express Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026

The wall you cannot reopen

Picture the day after tile goes up. The shower valve sits two inches too low for the new head, and nobody noticed until the finished wall was in front of you. Fixing it means breaking the tile you just paid to install.

That scenario is why a bathroom remodel starts on paper. The studs are open only once, and the plumber needs your answers while they are. If you want to talk a layout through, call (206) 864-6558 and describe what you plan to keep and what you plan to move.

Decisions in the order the work happens

Settle these in sequence, because each one limits the next.

01

Pick the fixtures

Toilet, vanity, tub or shower valve, and faucet models set the rough-in dimensions the plumber works to.

02

Fix the layout

Decide which fixtures stay where they are and which move. Every move adds work to drain, vent and supply.

03

Plan the drain route

The toilet and shower drains need a steady pitch to the main stack, and that can dictate where fixtures end up.

04

Set valve heights

Shower valve, handheld holder and showerhead positions all need to match your height and your tile pattern.

05

Test and inspect before closing

Pressure-test the supply lines and look at every joint before the drywall covers them.

Shower, tub or both: the plumbing difference

A walk-in shower needs a drain in the floor, a waterproof pan beneath the tile and a valve with the right trim depth. A tub needs an overflow, a drain assembly and access to the trap, often from below or through a panel on the far side of the wall.

A tub-to-shower conversion keeps some of those lines and changes others. The existing drain may sit in the wrong place for a larger shower base, and the floor under the tub often needs repair once the tub is out.

If you plan a body spray or a second showerhead, say so early. Extra outlets draw more water at once, and the supply lines and the valve must be sized to feed them without starving the main head.

Drain pitch and venting, in plain words

A drain line carries waste by gravity. It needs a gentle, steady downhill pitch. Too flat and solids sit and clog. Too steep and water outruns the solids and leaves them behind. The code sets the allowable pitch, and the plumber lays out the pipe to it.

A vent lets air behind the water so it flows without suction. Each fixture trap holds a seal of water against sewer gas, and a poor vent can pull that seal dry. If you are adding a fixture, ask where its vent will rise and whether it joins an existing stack.

A larger layout change can mean new lines through the floor or ceiling. That is where plumbing installation work moves from a fixture swap to a small project.

Questions to settle before demo day

  • ✓Where is the main shutoff? Know it, test it and label it before any wall is opened.
  • ✓What pipe material is in the walls? Galvanized steel, copper, PEX and cast iron all behave differently when cut into.
  • ✓Is the toilet drain moving? If so, confirm the new path and the pitch before ordering a vanity.
  • ✓Do the fixtures match the rough-in? Fixture models should be on site, not just ordered.
  • ✓Which stages get inspected? Ask the city permit center if the work needs a permit, then plan the schedule around inspection.
  • ✓Who covers hidden damage? Decide in advance what happens if the open wall shows rot or a failed pipe.

Old pipes show up at demo

Late 1940s and 1950s homes commonly had galvanized steel supply lines and cast iron or clay drains. Many have been partly replaced with copper or PEX since. Newer builds from the 2000s onward often use PEX.

A camera inspection and a pipe-material check before you commit will show what is in your walls. If the supply lines are old, the remodel is a good moment to talk about repiping the bathroom run while the wall is open.

When the work should stop

Pause the job and call if demo exposes a leaking joint, a corroded supply line or a drain that is cracked or sagging. Pause if you find water near a wet electrical box, and do not touch it.

If anyone smells gas during the work, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first, then call us. We do not service gas lines. Our line takes remodel questions too, and we also cover Lynnwood bathroom projects next door.
