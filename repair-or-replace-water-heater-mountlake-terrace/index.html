Water Heater Repair or Replace in Mountlake Terrace: How to Decide

Repairing or replacing a water heater in Mountlake Terrace comes down to three questions. What is failing, how old is the unit, and is the tank itself wet? The sections below sort your symptoms in about two minutes.

Fix the part or retire the tank

Replace the water heater when the tank itself is leaking. Repair it when a replaceable part has failed and the tank is dry and sound. Age and repeat failures settle the cases in between.

The tank is a steel shell with a glass-like lining. Once water works through a rusted seam or a failed weld, no part swap can seal it. A bad thermostat, heating element, igniter, thermocouple or T&P valve is different. Those parts come out and go in without touching the tank.

If you cannot tell which side you are on, call (206) 864-6558 and describe what you see. Our dispatcher can usually tell a part problem from a tank problem by the symptoms, and we give a clear price before work starts.

Reading the age off the label

Age is the tiebreaker, so find it before you decide anything. The data plate sits on the side of the tank near the top. The serial number usually carries the month and year of manufacture, and each maker publishes a decoder.

Many tank heaters reach the end of their useful life somewhere around a decade, sometimes a little longer when the anode rod was replaced on schedule. Past that mark, a repair often buys a short stretch before the next failure shows up.

A young heater with a failed part is a repair almost every time. An old heater with a failed part is a judgment call, and the question to ask is how many more months of trouble-free service the repair would realistically buy.

Signs it is the tank and not a part

Wipe every fitting dry first, then watch the unit for an hour. These clues point at the shell.

  • ✓Wet at the bottom seam. Moisture on the pan or floor with dry pipe fittings above it.
  • ✓Rust streaks on the jacket. Orange trails running down the outside of the tank.
  • ✓Hot water that stays rusty. The color does not clear after the line runs for a few minutes.
  • ✓A rotted drain pan. Soft, stained or collapsing material under the heater.
  • ✓Repeat visits. A different failure every few months on a unit that is already old.
  • ✓Rumbling that returns. Noise comes back soon after a flush on an aging tank.

What a repair can and cannot buy

Replacing an anode rod, flushing sediment and swapping an expansion tank can extend the life of a younger heater. Past about a decade, the lining has usually been attacked already, and a new rod rarely reverses that.

Spend repair money on parts that protect the tank, not on parts that sit beside a tank that is already failing.

If you replace, fix what caused the failure

A heater that died early may have been hurt by the setup around it. Closed plumbing systems can push pressure up when the water heats, and a missing or waterlogged expansion tank lets that pressure beat on the tank and the T&P valve.

Replacement is the time to check the discharge pipe, the supply stops, the venting on a gas unit, and the strapping that holds the tank to the wall. We also cover plumbing calls in neighboring Lynnwood, and the same checks apply there.

While you wait for the technician

A few steps limit the damage and speed up the visit.

Shut the cold supply

If the tank is leaking, turn the cold supply valve at the heater clockwise until it stops.

Kill the power

For an electric unit, switch off its breaker only if the floor around the panel is dry and safe.

Photograph the data plate

A clear picture of the label lets us read age, size and fuel type before we arrive.

Clear the space

Move boxes and stored items so there is room to work and a clear path to the unit.

Note what you saw

Write down where the water appeared and when the hot water changed.

When to stop and call right away

Some situations are not a decide-later call. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first, then call us. If water is spraying, the tank is bulging, or there is water near an electrical panel or outlet, do not touch anything and keep people away.

Scalding water at the tap, or steam from the discharge pipe, also needs a prompt visit. Our line is open at any hour, and you can read how water heater repair works while you wait.
