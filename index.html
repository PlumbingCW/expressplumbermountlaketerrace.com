Water is spreading right now: do these first

Stay calm, protect yourself first, and then protect the house. These steps are the same whether it is midnight or Saturday morning.

  • 1Shut off the water at the fixture's supply stop. If you cannot find it or it will not turn, close the main shutoff.
  • 2Look for anything electrical near the water. If the floor is wet around a panel, outlets or a water heater, do not touch it. Turn off a breaker only if the floor is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • 3If a smell of gas is present, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside before you call us.
  • 4Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet area and set a bucket under any drip.
  • 5Call (206) 864-6558 and tell the dispatcher what you see, where the shutoff is and whether the water has stopped.

How to tell an emergency from a problem that can wait

An emergency is water you cannot stop or sewage coming back into the house. A heater leaking into a closet or garage also qualifies, as does a sump pump that stopped while the pit fills. Standing water in an electrical room is serious even if the leak looks small.

A gurgling toilet with water at the baseboard is a warning that the main line may be blocked. A slow kitchen drain or a faucet drip is a normal call. When you are unsure, call anyway. The dispatcher would rather hear a false alarm than miss a real one.

The three calls we take most often

Clogged and backing-up drains. A single slow sink is usually a trap or branch clog. When the tub, toilet and washer drain all misbehave together, the main line is the likely culprit. Our drain cleaning service starts by finding which kind you have, and a camera on a flexible cable shows what is blocking the pipe.

Water heaters that leak or fail. Water around the base of the tank, a rotten-egg smell, rust at the fittings or no hot water at all all lead here. A leak at a fitting, a T&P valve or a failed element can often be repaired, and water heater repair is where that decision gets made.

Heaters past repair. A tank that leaks from the shell has failed, because corrosion inside cannot be patched. Water heater replacement covers sizing, venting, the expansion tank and strapping, since this is earthquake country.

A city of 21,286 people that runs its own water and sewer

Mountlake Terrace is in Snohomish County, incorporated in 1954, with a 2020 census population of 21,286 across about 4.16 square miles. The city itself provides water, sewer and stormwater utilities inside city limits, and bills every other month.

That matters when something goes wrong. A bill that jumps for no reason can point to a leak on your side of the meter, so read it when it arrives and compare it to the last one. If water is leaking from public pipes in the street, the city is who you report it to. If the pipe is inside your property, call us and we will help you work out which side it is.

Find these before you need them

Five minutes now saves a bad night later.

  • ✓The main shutoff. Locate it, label it and make sure everyone in the house knows where it is.
  • ✓Fixture supply stops. Check the ones under sinks and behind toilets. Stiff valves are better found before a leak.
  • ✓The water heater's cold supply valve. Closing it stops the tank from refilling during a leak.
  • ✓Your sewer cleanout. A capped pipe near the foundation or in the yard. Crews need access to it.
  • ✓A flashlight and a bucket. Power may be off, and drips need catching.
