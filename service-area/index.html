The city we are based in

Mountlake Terrace sits in Snohomish County with about 21,000 residents and roughly 9,200 housing units, according to the 2020 census. About 59 percent of occupied homes are owner-occupied and 41 percent are rented, so we take calls from homeowners, tenants and landlords. If you rent, the repair may be your landlord's responsibility, so tell them early and call us if they ask for a plumber.

Housing here ranges from the 1949 cinderblock homes that the original developers built on a former landing field to apartments from the 1970s and new mixed-use buildings in Town Center between 220th and 236th Streets. Older homes tend to bring drain and water heater calls. Newer buildings bring fixture and commercial calls.

What to tell us about your location

Calling from outside the city limits works exactly the same way. A few location details help the dispatcher plan the visit.

Your town and street

Give the full address, including the city name, since several ZIP codes in the area share a city name with a neighbor.

Cross street or landmark

A cross street helps when the house is on a long road or a cul-de-sac that navigation apps misplace.

House or unit type

Single-family home, townhouse, apartment or condo. Shared walls and shared shutoffs change the first steps.

Where the shutoff is

If you already found the main valve, say so. If not, the dispatcher can talk you through finding it.

Who to call for a street-side problem

Inside Mountlake Terrace, the city provides water, sewer and stormwater service, and Public Works handles water or sewer line breaks at 425-670-8264 on weekday days. After hours, the Snohomish County non-emergency line is 425-407-3999. Other towns have their own providers, so check your bill. Anything past your meter or cleanout on your own property is where a plumber comes in, and that is the call to (206) 864-6558.

City lines, county lines and permits

Mountlake Terrace and Lynnwood are in Snohomish County, while Kenmore and part of Bothell reach into King County. That matters mostly for paperwork, because each city runs its own permit office. When a job needs a permit, such as a water heater replacement or a sewer line repair, the plumber will tell you which office applies and what to ask it. For work inside Mountlake Terrace, the permit center at City Hall, 23204 58th Ave W, takes calls at 425-776-1161.

If you rent, check your lease before you call anyone. Many leases route plumbing repairs through the landlord or property manager first. We can speak with the manager if you want us to, and you can reach the dispatcher any hour at (206) 864-6558.
