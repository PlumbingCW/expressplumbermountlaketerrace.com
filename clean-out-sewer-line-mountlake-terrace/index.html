Where Is Your Mountlake Terrace Sewer Cleanout, and When to Use It

Every house has a way into its sewer line, and most owners have never looked for it. This Mountlake Terrace guide shows where a cleanout hides, what a blocked main looks like, and how cable and jetting differ.

Call (206) 864-6558
By the Mountlake Terrace Express Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026

A way into the pipe you never see

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives access to the sewer line without removing a toilet. It usually looks like a three or four inch round plug, either threaded or with a square nut on top. It may sit in the basement, the garage, a crawlspace, a yard box or near the foundation outside.

That cap is the first tool a plumber uses on a main line blockage. Cleaning from the cleanout is faster and cleaner than pulling a toilet, and it lets a cable run straight into the main. Finding yours before a backup saves time on a bad day.

How to locate your cleanout

Start where the main line leaves the house and work outward.

01

Check the lowest level first

Look along basement walls, near the main stack and by the water heater for a capped pipe at floor level.

02

Walk the foundation outside

Look for a round cap in the ground or a green or black lid in the lawn, usually on the side nearest the street.

03

Follow the main drain

Trace where the largest drain leaves the house. The cleanout often sits along that run.

04

Look in the garage and crawlspace

Older homes sometimes route the access to those spots.

05

Photograph and note the spot

Keep the picture for the dispatcher. Do not remove the cap.

Signs the blockage is in the main

A single slow sink is a fixture problem. These signs say the main line is struggling.

  • ✓More than one fixture is slow. Tub, toilets and the washer drain at once.
  • ✓The lowest drain backs up first. A basement floor drain or tub is often the first to show water.
  • ✓Gurgling after you flush. Air is pushed back through traps.
  • ✓Sewer smell with no clear source. A partial clog forces gas past dry traps.
  • ✓Toilet water rises when the washer drains. The shared line is carrying more than it can pass.
  • ✓Repeat clogs after a clear. Roots or buildup are still in the pipe.

Roots, the usual repeat offender

Tree roots follow moisture. A joint in clay or cast iron pipe, or a loose connection in a newer line, gives fine root hairs a way in. They grow into a mat that catches paper and grease, and the line clogs again.

A cutting head opens the path, but the roots can grow back. A camera shows whether the pipe is cracked or offset where roots enter. If it is, cleaning only buys time, and a repair discussion is the honest next step. Our sewer line cleaning service starts with what the line is telling us.

What to do while the line is backing up

Do these in order and stay out of standing sewage.

  • 1Stop using water, including toilets, dishwashers and laundry.
  • 2Keep people and pets out of wet areas and wear gloves if you must enter.
  • 3Locate the cleanout and leave the cap in place.
  • 4Call (206) 864-6558 and describe which fixtures are backing up.
  • 5If you see water in the street or neighbors report the same, call City Public Works or the county line.

What a cleaning visit looks like

We start by asking which drains are affected and where the cleanout sits. A cable goes in through the cleanout and runs toward the blockage until the motor loads up. The cutting head clears the obstruction, and then we run water to confirm the line drains.

Afterward you should know what was in the pipe. Grease, paper and roots call for different follow-up, and a camera pass can confirm the line is open end to end. You get a clear price before work starts, so there is no guessing at the end.

Do not use acid or strong openers

Chemical openers can sit in a blocked pipe, burn skin if the line backs up and harm older metal. They do not clear roots. A cable or jetting tool is the right fix.

When a camera makes sense after the clear

If the line clears and the problem returns, or if the house is old, a camera look is a good idea. It shows the pipe wall, offsets, roots and sags. The result tells you whether a cleaning plan is enough or whether a section needs repair.

We cannot service gas lines. If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 first. Otherwise call (206) 864-6558 and the dispatcher will give a clear price before work starts. We also work with homeowners in Lynnwood next door.
